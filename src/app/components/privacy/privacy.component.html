What this is

Xomware is a personal portfolio of small apps run by Dominick Giordano (handle @domgiordano). This page describes what data Xomware collects when you use any app under xomware.com or any sub-app such as xomappetit.xomware.com.

What we collect

  • Email address — required to create an account.
  • Handle (e.g. @domgiordano) — chosen by you at sign-up.
  • Display name and profile picture — if you sign in with Google, we store the name and avatar Google returns. You can change either at any time on the profile page.
  • Sign-in / sign-up timestamps — we keep a small audit log so the operator (Dominick) can see usage. Rows expire automatically after 90 days.
  • Pages you visit on xomware.com — the path (e.g. /apps), the time, which app link you clicked through to, your browser's user-agent string, and any error the site hits while you are using it. This is stored on Xomware's own servers, expires after 90 days, and is used only to see which apps people are interested in and to find bugs. You do not need an account for this to be recorded — see Cookies and tracking below, including how to turn it off.
  • App-specific content you create — e.g. recipes you save in Xom Appétit, files you edit, or any other data you enter into a Xomware app.

How we store it

Account data lives in AWS Cognito (identity provider) and AWS DynamoDB (profile + app data), encrypted at rest with AWS KMS. Avatars live in S3 behind CloudFront. Everything is hosted in us-east-1.

Who we share it with

Nobody. Xomware does not sell, rent, or share your data. The only third parties that touch your data are AWS (the hosting provider) and, if you choose Google sign-in, Google (purely to authenticate you — we receive your email + display name + avatar URL and nothing else).

Cookies and tracking

Xomware uses session tokens issued by Cognito to keep you signed in. There is no third-party advertising, no cross-site tracking, and no analytics product — no Google Analytics, no ad pixels, nothing that sends your browsing to anyone else. The activity described above goes to Xomware's own servers and stays there.

To count visitors without accounts, the site stores a random identifier in your browser's local storage. It is a random string, it is not linked to anything about you, and it is only readable by xomware.com. If you later sign in, that identifier is associated with your account so the operator can see the visit that led to a sign-up as one session rather than two.

How to turn it off. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or “Do Not Track”, Xomware records nothing at all — no identifier is created and no request is sent. Most privacy-focused browsers and extensions send one of these by default, and both are a setting in your browser. Clearing your site data also removes the identifier.

Your rights

You can delete your account at any time from the profile page, which removes your row from the identity pool and your profile data from DynamoDB. App-specific content (recipes, etc.) is removed as part of account deletion. Activity rows are not deleted immediately — they expire on their own within 90 days — but you can email to have them removed sooner. To request a copy of your data or ask any other question, email dominickj.giordano@gmail.com.

Changes

If this policy changes, the “Last updated” date above will change with it. Material changes will also be announced on the home page.
